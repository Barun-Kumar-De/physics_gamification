Draw acceleration → get velocity & displacement via numerical integration

CLASS XI · MOTION IN A STRAIGHT LINE
Step 1 — Draw the Acceleration Curve
  • 1Click and drag on the Acceleration graph (top-left) to draw a straight-line segment. The x-axis is time (s) and the y-axis is acceleration (m/s²).
  • 2Both axes snap to integer values only — time snaps to whole seconds, acceleration snaps to whole m/s².
  • 3Draw multiple segments end-to-end to build a piecewise linear acceleration profile. Each new segment connects to the previous one.
  • 4Use the LOAD EXAMPLE button to see a pre-built multi-segment profile.
  • 5Use CLEAR to erase everything and start over.
Step 2 — Configure Axes & Initial Conditions
  • 1Set T max (5 s / 10 s / 20 s) to control how long the time axis is before drawing.
  • 2Set a range (±5, ±10, ±20 m/s²) to control the vertical scale of the acceleration graph.
  • 3Enter v₀ — the initial velocity at t = 0. Default is 0 m/s. Changing it instantly recomputes all graphs.
Step 3 — Integrate to Get Velocity & Displacement
  • 1Click INTEGRATE → (or just finish drawing — it auto-integrates). The Velocity graph is computed via v(t) = v₀ + ∫a dt.
  • 2The Displacement graph is then computed via x(t) = ∫v dt with x₀ = 0.
  • 3All three graphs share the same time axis, so you can read off corresponding values at any moment.
Step 4 — Read the Section Analysis
  • 1After integrating, the graphs are divided into colour-coded sections (S1, S2, S3…). Each section spans between two consecutive time points you chose.
  • 2A Section-by-Section Analysis panel appears below the graphs. Click each section tab (S1, S2…) to see its breakdown.
  • 3Each section shows three rows:
      • Acceleration — what the acceleration is doing (constant, increasing, decreasing, crossing zero).
      • Velocity — how velocity responds (linear rise/fall, curve, local max/min at zero-crossing).
      • Displacement — how position changes (linear, concave up/down, local max/min where v = 0).
  • 4The Δv and Δx badges show the net change in velocity and displacement over each section.
Key Physics Reminders
  • →Where a = constant > 0: velocity rises linearly (straight line with positive slope).
  • →Where a is increasing: velocity curve bends upward (concave up, slope grows).
  • →Where a crosses zero (+ → −): velocity has a local maximum at that exact time.
  • →Where a crosses zero (− → +): velocity has a local minimum at that exact time.
  • →Where v crosses zero (+ → −): displacement has a local maximum at that time.
  • →Where v = constant: displacement increases linearly (straight line).
